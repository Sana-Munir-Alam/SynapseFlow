## Dashboard

A single landing page after login: study streak, MCQ accuracy, schedule completion, and badges earned, plus quick links into groups, upcoming events, and every other section of the app. Everything on this page is computed from real data — nothing here is a static placeholder.

<img src="assets/dashboard.png" width="800" alt="Dashboard" />

## Notes & course files

Each course is a container for uploaded files, generated flashcards, and generated MCQs.

<img src="assets/notes-courses.png" width="800" alt="Notes and course list" />
<img src="assets/create-course.png" width="500" alt="Create a new course" />

Files upload as PDF or DOCX. On upload, the backend verifies the actual file bytes match the declared type (not just the filename or the browser-supplied mimetype), then extracts and embeds the text in the background so it's ready for AI grounding without blocking the upload response.

<img src="assets/upload-documents.png" width="800" alt="Uploading a document" />
<img src="assets/preview-document.png" width="800" alt="Previewing an uploaded document" />

PDFs are rendered page by page with `react-pdf`; DOCX files are converted to HTML in the browser with `mammoth`. Neither requires downloading the file to view it outside the app.

## Flashcards

Flashcards are generated from a course's uploaded files by Gemini, distributed across however many files are in the course. They're cached against the course's `updatedAt` timestamp — reopening a course that hasn't changed shows the existing cards instantly instead of regenerating them, which is both faster and cheaper.

<img src="assets/generate-flashcards.png" width="800" alt="Generating flashcards" />
<img src="assets/sample-flashcard.png" width="500" alt="A flashcard" />
<img src="assets/flashcard-solved.png" width="500" alt="Flashcard marked familiar or unfamiliar" />

Each card can be read aloud, and cards can be rated familiar/unfamiliar, which feeds directly into the Progress page's flashcard mastery number.

## MCQ tests

Same generation pipeline as flashcards, applied to multiple-choice questions instead: four options, one correct answer, and an AI-written explanation shown after the question is answered.

<img src="assets/generate-mcqs.png" width="800" alt="Generating MCQs" />
<img src="assets/sample-mcqs.png" width="800" alt="An MCQ question" />
<img src="assets/mcq-solved.png" width="800" alt="MCQ answered with explanation" />

Every attempt is recorded per user, which is what powers the MCQ accuracy percentage on both the Dashboard and Progress page.

## Group chat & the AI agent

Real-time group chat per course group, over Socket.IO. Two commands bring the AI into the conversation directly:

- **`@ai`** — general questions, or `@ai summarize 10` to summarize the last N messages in the group.
- **`@docs`** — asks the same AI, but grounded in the group members' uploaded course files (retrieval-augmented generation), with the answer citing which file and page it came from.

<img src="assets/group-chat.png" width="800" alt="Group chat with the AI agent" />

Messages support Markdown and LaTeX math rendering, a copy button, and a read-aloud button (see [Voice input and read aloud](#voice-input-and-read-aloud)). Users can delete their own messages for themselves or for everyone; group admins can delete anyone's message.

## Collaborative whiteboard

A live, multi-user whiteboard per group, built on [tldraw](https://tldraw.dev). Two people in the same group can draw, move, and edit the same board at the same time, with each other's cursors visible live.

<img src="assets/whiteboard-realtime-collab.png" width="800" alt="Two users editing the same whiteboard simultaneously" />

The whiteboard connects over a raw WebSocket (`ws`), separate from the Socket.IO connection used for chat, and authenticates using the same JWT cookie as the rest of the app rather than a token in the URL.

## Scheduler & AI study plan

The scheduler tracks assignments, quizzes, exams, and projects, and generates a weekly study plan across a student's courses.

<img src="assets/scheduler.png" width="800" alt="Scheduler with weekly study plan" />

The AI study plan is generated by Gemini from the courses added, each course's preparation level and priority, and existing deadlines — the result is a full weekly hour breakdown per course, editable afterward. Students log each planned session as complete, missed, less than planned, or more than planned:

<img src="assets/mark-schedule-status.png" width="600" alt="Marking a scheduled session's status" />

Deadlines can also be synced in from Google Calendar via OAuth, with event titles automatically matched to a type (assignment, quiz, midterm, final, project, study, or general) by keyword.

## Progress tracking

Study completion, MCQ accuracy, flashcard mastery, current and best streaks, and badges, all computed from real session and attempt data rather than being separately tracked counters.

<img src="assets/progress-overview.png" width="800" alt="Progress overview" />
<img src="assets/progress-activity.png" width="800" alt="Activity and performance breakdown" />
<img src="assets/performance-badges.png" width="500" alt="Performance badges" />

The streak rule is explicit in the UI: a calendar day only counts toward a streak if every session scheduled for that day was completed or exceeded — a day with even one missed session breaks it.

## Notifications

Two kinds of reminder, both delivered as an in-app notification and a matching email:

- **Deadline reminders** — sent the day before an assignment, quiz, midterm, final, or project is due. General and study-type calendar entries deliberately don't trigger this, since they aren't graded deadlines.
- **Falling-behind reminders** — sent when a scheduled study session gets logged as missed or as less than the planned hours.

<img src="assets/notifications-panel.png" width="400" alt="Notifications panel" />

Notifications are pushed to the client the moment they're created, over a per-user Socket.IO room, rather than the client polling for them — a REST fetch still runs on page load and on reconnect as a fallback, in case a notification was created while the client was offline.

## Voice input and read aloud

The AI chatbot accepts spoken input: recording is sent to Gemini as audio, which transcribes English, Urdu, or a mix of both in the same call — no separate speech-to-text service.

<img src="assets/chatbot-voice-input.png" width="500" alt="Recording a voice message to the AI chatbot" />

Recording can be canceled before sending (the send button becomes a cancel button while recording), and a recording with no actual speech in it is correctly reported as such instead of the model inventing words to fill the silence:

<img src="assets/chatbot-no-speech-detected.png" width="500" alt="No speech detected message" />

Read-aloud on AI responses uses ElevenLabs text-to-speech rather than the browser's built-in speech synthesis — see [`docs/architecture.md`](architecture.md#text-to-speech) for why. A shared player utility ensures only one message plays at a time across the whole app.

## Low-bandwidth mode

A setting that reduces background data usage for students on a slow or metered connection.

With it on, notifications stop refetching on window focus, and two of the heavier features ask for confirmation before loading instead of loading automatically:

## Urdu support

Every AI-facing feature in the app detects and responds in whichever language — English or Urdu, or a mix — the input is in, rather than defaulting to English or requiring a language switch. This isn't a separate "Urdu mode"; it's the same feature set, just language-aware.

<img src="assets/urdu/urdu-file-upload.png" width="700" alt="Uploading an Urdu document" />
<img src="assets/urdu/urdu-file-preview.png" width="700" alt="Previewing an Urdu document" />

Flashcards and MCQs are generated in whichever language the source material is written in:

<img src="assets/urdu/urdu-generated-flashcard.png" width="500" alt="Urdu flashcard" />
<img src="assets/urdu/urdu-flashcard-answer.png" width="500" alt="Urdu flashcard answer" />
<img src="assets/urdu/urdu-generated-mcq.png" width="600" alt="Urdu MCQ" />
<img src="assets/urdu/urdu-mcq-wrong.png" width="600" alt="Urdu MCQ, incorrect answer" />
<img src="assets/urdu/urdu-mcq-complete.png" width="600" alt="Urdu MCQ test completed" />

The AI chatbot and group chat's `@docs` command answer in Urdu when asked in Urdu, grounded in the same uploaded documents as the English flow:

<img src="assets/urdu/urdu-ai-chat-docs.png" width="800" alt="AI answering a document-grounded question in Urdu" />

Urdu text throughout the app renders in the Noto Nastaliq Urdu font rather than falling back to whatever generic Arabic-script font happens to be installed on the viewer's device.
